## Goal

## Accomplished

### PR #162 review — posted, verdict approve and merge

Reviewed at **895ef65** (the PR head; my first pass was against a stale worktree
at 6682f48, 27 files / +38k lines behind, and everything said before the posted
comment was withdrawn). Verification: `cargo test --release` → **90 test
binaries, 848 passed, 0 failed, 21 ignored**; `gh pr checks 162` all green.

Five findings, none blocking:

1. **`ftran_sparse`/`btran_sparse` have no density guard.** The docs call this a
   caller-routes-it design choice, but the motivating caller (a dual simplex)
   cannot know the density of `α = B⁻¹A_q` until after the solve that produces
   it. Measured on 14 QPLIB instances: log-log Pearson **r = −0.944** between
   `α` density and `api/off` speedup. `α` under 10% dense → **1.167x** (n=10);
   at or above 10% → **0.837x** (n=4). An ideal guard would take the overall
   1.061x to **1.116x**, i.e. the missing guard costs about half of what the API
   is worth — and the threshold that would fix it is feral's own shipped
   `hyper_sparse_max_density`.
2. The sparse-rhs API is **not** bit-identical to the dense entry points (1e-9)
   while the reach route **is** (`assert_eq!`); the doc comment does not say so,
   and #163 is the standing proof that a rounding-trajectory change matters.
3. `SingularBasis { column }` reports a permuted pivot position in the solve
   path (`sparse_hyper.rs:389,391,420,422`) where `sparse_factor.rs` reports the
   original basis column — the same class of defect a91519d just fixed.
4. **#163's ordering revert costs 4.3-12.4x on the analysis, unstated.** See
   below; this was the largest measured effect on the branch in either direction.
5. Scope note: the title says "reach-limited sweeps + sparse-rhs API" but the
   branch also carries #163, which reverts #160's headline default.

### #1008 — the answer is *no, do not close it*, and the deficiency is the ordering

Baseline gap, discopt vs HiGHS on 15 QPLIB relaxation LPs: **11.95x geomean,
12.68x median, up to 25.22x** = 1.40x more pivots × 6.84x per-iteration cost.

**Kernel attribution** (14 instances, per-instance median | pooled %):
DualFtranAlpha 21.53 | 19.84, DualBtranRho 18.81 | 16.97, LuNumeric 7.82 | 13.44,
DualPrice 7.78 | 7.11, DualRcUpdate 6.29 | 6.67, DualRatio 4.72 | 5.25,
DualDevex 3.50 | 3.01, LuSymbolic 2.97 | 3.21, other/untimed 25.17 | 24.51.
**feral-owned ≈ 53%**, discopt-owned ≈ 22%. (This corrects an earlier
"PRICE dominates" reading drawn from QPLIB_1157 alone.)

**Density census** (15 instances): `rho` median **0.150%** dense
(0.008-14.46%); `alpha` median **6.565%** (1.211-61.95%); `alpha_r` ~0.156%.

**Definitive 4-arm panel** — 14 instances, REPS=2, all arms interleaved inside
**one binary** (md5 `cc1e146`, single driver PID, `_ARM.txt` stamped with the
resolved `cargo tree` line), objectives agreeing to 9 decimal places:

| arm | geomean | median | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `api` (sparse-rhs API — all of PR #162's solve work) | 1.067x | 1.090x | 0.726x | 1.363x |
| `tri` (`analyze_triangularized` instead of `analyze`) | **1.306x** | 1.469x | 0.389x | 1.674x |
| `all` (both) | **1.497x** | 1.729x | 0.307x | 2.584x |

Excluding QPLIB_2055: api 1.099x, tri 1.433x, all **1.691x**. This takes
discopt's gap to HiGHS from **14.4x to 9.6x** geomean (14.9x → 8.8x ex-2055).

The ordering is worth **4.5x more than everything PR #162 does to the solves**,
and the two compose. Neither requires a feral code change — both orderings and
both solve paths ship in the branch under review.

Why the ordering is first-order rather than amortizable: `SparseLuSymbolic::analyze`
is re-run on **every** refactorization. Measured in feral alone, 20 reps, release,
no caller involved:

| basis | m | nnz | `analyze` | `analyze_triangularized` | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QPLIB_1157 | 3937 | 29376 | 10.329 ms | 0.834 ms | **12.4x** |
| QPLIB_3852 | 1760 | 4003 | 0.247 ms | 0.038 ms | 6.6x |
| bchoco06_illcond | 833 | 2404 | 0.148 ms | 0.034 ms | 4.3x |

On QPLIB_3775 in full: `LuSymbolic` **1048.5 ms across 64 factorizations** vs
`LuNumeric` **184.6 ms** — 5.7x the numeric factorization spent choosing a column
order. `tri=1` takes that LP from **2.215 s to 1.193 s** with an identical
objective and an identical factorization count.

The sparse-rhs API does its job on the side it was aimed at: `DualBtranRho` drops
from **18.81% to 0.97%** of the loop. `DualFtranAlpha` barely moves (21.53% →
20.72%) — that is finding 1, the missing density guard, showing up in the profile.

**QPLIB_2055 is the honest counterexample.** Under `tri` it is **0.389x** — a 2.6x
slowdown — and its objective moves in the 9th significant figure
(2314023.629152459 vs …471), i.e. the #163 trajectory hazard reproducing in the
opposite direction. It is also the panel's densest `alpha` (61.95%). The
conclusion is *not* "revert the revert"; it is that the peel must be a
per-caller opt-in with a documented cost and a fallback for this class.

## Next Session Should

1. **Add the density guard to `ftran_sparse`/`btran_sparse`** (review finding 1).
   The reach is already computed inside the solve, so the information is in hand
   by the time the cost becomes knowable: when
   `order.len() > hyper_sparse_max_density * m`, fall back to the dense sweep and
   emit the nonzeros. Worth ~1.061x → ~1.116x on the panel, and it stops every
   caller having to rediscover the cliff.
2. **Document the ordering tradeoff in both directions.** The `analyze` doc
   comment argues the accuracy case at length and never states the 4.3-12.4x it
   hands back. Consider promoting the choice to an `LuParams`/analysis knob with
   a documented default instead of two separately-named constructors, so callers
   can A/B it without a code change — which is how it had to be measured here.
3. **Run down QPLIB_3225.** It solves under a feral built from `main` but fails
   under the #162 branch in **all** arms including `off`, so it is neither the
   reach route nor the sparse API; the only remaining difference is #163's
   ordering revert. Now directly testable with `DISCOPT_LU_TRIANGULARIZE=1`.
   If confirmed, the revert bought bchoco06 and lost QPLIB_3225.
4. **Investigate why `analyze` is 4-12x slower than the peel + AMD-on-bump.**
   The peel shrinks the AMD input, but a 12.4x gap on m=3937 suggests the AMD on
   the full basis is also doing avoidable work. A cheaper `analyze` would make
   the whole tradeoff moot.
5. Leave #1008 **open on the feral side** until items 1-2 land: the solve side is
   essentially finished, but the shipped default ordering is a measured 1.306x
   geomean deficiency in a real caller.
