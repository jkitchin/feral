## Accomplished

### The gather/scatter split, which is the whole of issue #161B

Code inspection of the four sparse triangular kernels explains the issue's
headline number exactly. Two are **scatter** form (`L y = s`, `Uᵀ z = s`) and
already test for zero before doing work; two are **gather** form (`U w = s`,
`Lᵀ v = s`) and read every row of `U` / every column of `L` regardless. Issue
#161 measured a one-nonzero solution costing **0.74x** a fully dense one — half
the solve going to ~0 and half staying at full cost is what predicts a number
between 0.5x and 1.0x rather than 1.0x.

So the fix is specifically to the two gather kernels, and the prediction is that
it moves the sparse-rhs case and leaves the dense-rhs case alone.

### What landed

- `usolve` and `lt_solve` now compute the reach of the right-hand side's pattern
  in the factor's DAG and sweep only those positions, behind a density cap.
  `usolve` walks `u_above`, which the Forrest–Tomlin update already builds and
  maintains — nothing new was needed. `lt_solve` needed a row-wise index of `L`,
  built at factor time and valid for the life of the factor because the FT
  update never touches the base `L`.
- `ut_solve` hoists its `s[i] == 0.0` test above the `u_rows[i]` dereference. It
  was already flop-proportional but paid `m` cache misses per solve chasing one
  heap allocation per row. Unconditional; no arithmetic change.
- `LuParams::hyper_sparse_max_density`, default `0.25`, validated to `[0, 1]`.
  `0.0` restores the previous solve exactly and allocates none of the new state.
- `SparseLu::hyper_sparse_sweeps()` / `hyper_sparse_nodes()`.
- `tests/lu_hyper_sparse.rs` (9 tests), `examples/hyper_sparse_solve.rs`.
- Part 2 (below): `src/lu/sparse_hyper.rs`, `tests/lu_sparse_rhs.rs` (9 tests),
  Python bindings and 3 Python tests.

### Measured

Interleaved A/B, two factors of one basis differing only in the cap, arm order
alternating; `m = 4000`, 1.90 nnz/col, 400-column bump, fill 4.05x; unit-vector
right-hand sides, 1280 calls per arm.

```
                  ftran                     btran
  dense sweep     mean 68.3  p50 60.1 us    mean 57.5  p50 51.3 us
  reach-limited   mean 35.0  p50 23.5 us    mean 33.5  p50 23.9 us
  -->             mean 1.95x p50 2.56x      mean 1.72x p50 2.15x
  dense-rhs fallback: 0.97x      max |reach - dense| = 0.000e0
```

The two routes agree **bit for bit**, as they should — the omitted terms are
exactly `v * 0.0`.

Both mean and median are reported because this fixture's solution density is
bimodal (`ftran(eᵢ)` p50 = 3 nonzeros, p90 = 366). The mean is set by the
minority of solves that reach through the whole bump and *understates* the
hyper-sparse case; the median overstates it for a mixed workload.

The 0.97x on a dense right-hand side is the prediction above coming out right:
the sparse case moved, the dense case did not.

### The default was swept, not guessed

| cap | ftran | positions/sweep | dense-rhs fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 2.08x | 3.1 | 0.98x |
| 0.10 | 2.06x | 32.8 | 0.97x |
| **0.25** | **2.04x** | **39.5** | **0.98x** |
| 0.50 | 2.04x | 39.5 | 0.93x |
| 1.00 | 1.98x | 133.8 | 0.67x |

The win is flat across the range; what the cap controls is the downside. At 1.00
the cap can never be exceeded, so nothing ever aborts and a dense right-hand
side pays a full wasted reach — 0.67x. That row is the abort mechanism earning
its place.

### The vacuity counter caught a real no-op on its first run

All nine differential tests passed on correctness while
`hyper_sparse_sweeps()` was **0**: `reach_cap()` tested `self.reach` to decide
whether the workspace was live, but the solve entry points `mem::take` that
workspace out of `self` for the duration of the call, so the test always saw the
empty `Default` and disabled the route on every solve. Every correctness
assertion still passed, because the dense fallback is correct.

Without the counter this ships as a no-op with a green suite. That is the entire
argument for having it, and it is the same argument PR #160 makes for
`used_dense_bump()`.

### Part 2: the `O(m)` floor, removed

The first half of the session landed the reach-limited sweeps and stopped at an
`O(m)` floor, recording it as a known limit and naming `ftran_sparse` as the
follow-up. That follow-up was then requested and is in the same branch.

The floor was never a removable term — a phase probe showed 22 us on a
3.4-position reach spread evenly over ~6 linear passes. Every one of those passes
exists because `ftran(&mut self, rhs: &mut [f64])` hands the solve a dense vector,
which is the only description of the input it has. So the signature changed:

```rust
pub fn ftran_sparse(&mut self, rhs: &[(usize, f64)], out: &mut Vec<(usize, f64)>)
    -> Result<(), FeralError>;
```

Nothing in the path is proportional to `m`. Measured, unit-vector right-hand
sides, `ftran` p50, alongside the deterministic operation count:

| `m` | dense sweep | reach-limited | **sparse API** | sparse work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 7.8 us | 4.7 us | **0.30 us** | 12.5 |
| 4,000 | 40.8 us | 20.3 us | **1.07 us** | 12.1 |
| 16,000 | 183.4 us | 89.6 us | **2.47 us** | 13.1 |
| 64,000 | 1251.0 us | 616.7 us | **3.34 us** | 11.0 |

**The work column is the result.** `m` grows 64x and the operation count does not
move; the dense sweep grows 160x, which is what an `O(m)` term looks like. At
`m = 64000` the sparse API is **277x the dense sweep on ftran, 234x on btran**.

Wall-clock still grows 11x over that 64x range, and that is *not* a residual
`O(m)` term: it decelerates (4x in `m` buys 2.3x from 4k to 16k but 1.35x from
16k to 64k), which is a cache/TLB plateau — the accumulator is 512 KB at
`m = 64000`, past L2, so each of the ~12 scattered accesses is a miss. That is
`O(work x miss cost)`.

This is why `last_sparse_solve_work()` exists and is asserted on rather than a
timing: a reintroduced `O(m)` term at these sizes would look like a constant
factor, indistinguishable from noise.

Python bindings were added too — issue #161's reproducer is Python, so a
Rust-only fix is not reachable from it. 68 passed, 1 skipped, including three
new tests.

### The composition needed a test, and got one

A clean textual merge is not evidence of a clean semantic one, and there is a
real interaction: #160's dense-bump route **splices a dense factorization into
the sparse `L` and `U`**, and the reach-limited sweeps navigate exactly those
structures (`u_above` for the `U` sweep, the row-wise `L` index for `Lᵀ`). A
splice producing entries those indices did not describe would truncate a reach
and return a wrong answer with no other symptom.

Neither branch could have caught it — part A predates the reach work, part B was
written against a tree without the splice. So:

- `reach_route_composes_with_the_dense_bump_route` (`tests/lu_hyper_sparse.rs`)
- `sparse_solves_compose_with_the_dense_bump_route` (`tests/lu_sparse_rhs.rs`)

Both assert `used_dense_bump()` alongside their own route's counter, so neither
half can be vacuously absent. Both pass.

## Two loose ends from the #160 review, closed

Both were carried in #160's own "next session should" and are now on main.

**1. `examples/basis_refactor.rs` reported population, not sample, standard
deviation.** The divisor `n.max(2.0 - 1.0)` parses as `n.max(1.0)` — `n` for any
real input — so the Bessel correction was absent.

This reaches beyond the harness: those `±` are published, in the merged #160
body and in `CHANGELOG.md`. At the 15 reps that run used, the correction factor
is `sqrt(15/14) = 1.0351`, so `9.837 ± 0.285` is really `9.837 ± 0.295` and
`0.851 ± 0.036` is `0.851 ± 0.037`. The changelog is corrected and carries a
`Fixed` entry saying where the numbers came from; the PR body is immutable
history and is not.

The correction is **analytic, not a re-measurement**, and is labelled as such:
the code computed `sqrt(Σ/n)` where it should have computed `sqrt(Σ/(n-1))`, so
the true value is the reported one times `sqrt(n/(n-1))` exactly. Re-running was
not an option regardless — the QPLIB basis behind those numbers is not in this
repository. Only the `±` move; the means, the speedups, and every conclusion
drawn from them are untouched, because the divisor never entered them.

Checked whether the typo was copy-pasted: it was not. The nine other
`2.0 - 1.0` hits in the tree are all legitimate `rng.unit() * 2.0 - 1.0` range
scalings.

**Why it could ship.** `cargo test` compiles examples but does not run
`#[test]`s inside them, so a benchmark harness's own arithmetic was unguarded.
CI gained a `cargo test --examples` step, and `mean_sd` gained unit tests
against the textbook sample `[2,4,4,4,5,5,7,9]` — population sd exactly 2.0,
sample sd `sqrt(32/7)`. The two differ by precisely the error in question, so
the test fails against the pre-fix code. `cargo test --examples` was verified
clean before the CI step was added, so it is not smuggling in a broken gate.

**2. `used_dense_bump` carried `factor_nnz`'s doc comment**, and `factor_nnz`
had none. A stray line, but it ships in the public rustdoc — where the first
line is the summary in the method list, so `used_dense_bump` was advertised as
returning a nonzero count. Moved to `factor_nnz`; both now have real docs, and
`used_dense_bump`'s explains why a test must assert on it (the route falls back
silently). `cargo doc --no-deps` emits no warnings for any `lu/` file.

## Decisions Made

- **The LU solve's singularity guard now covers the reached rows, not all rows.**
  Appended to `dev/decisions.md` with the soundness argument. Short version: a
  skipped row would have been assigned `0 / U[k,k]`, which is `0` for a healthy
  pivot and a valid component of an underdetermined consistent row for a zero
  one — so no answer changes. What narrows is an incidental diagnostic that
  factor and update time still provide against the pivot tolerance. Both L10
  tests still pass unchanged.

## Abandoned Approaches

- **Sparse permuted marshalling around the core solve.** Appended to
  `dev/tried-and-rejected.md` with the A/B. The hypothesis — that the four
  permuted gather/scatters were the next `O(m)` term because every access is
  random — was wrong: the access is random *within a 32 KB buffer*, which is
  L1/L2-resident, so it was never paying the misses the reasoning assumed. The
  arms straddled each other. Reverted.

## Known limits of what landed

- **On dense solutions the sparse API is neutral-to-slower.** It sorts its reach
  where the dense sweep walks the factor in order. On the mixed-density bump
  fixture it is 69x on the median solve but 6.5x on the mean, because the
  minority of solves that reach through the whole bump dominate the mean. The
  doc comments tell callers to keep using `ftran` there rather than leaving it
  to be discovered.
- **The eta replay is `O(eta ops)`, not `O(work)`.** Every op is walked whether
  or not its source is nonzero. Skipping zero sources is two lines and was
  deliberately not done: it diverges from `FtEta::apply_forward` on a non-finite
  multiplier (`mult * 0.0` is `NaN`) and changes no asymptotics. An indexed eta
  structure would; that is separate work.
- The measurements are on a synthetic LP-shaped fixture, not on the QPLIB bases
  issue #161 reports. Those are `.npz` files in the discopt tree and are not in
  this repo. The fixture reproduces the structural signature (near-triangular,
  ~2 nnz/col, non-triangular bump) but the transfer to the real bases is
  **unverified from here**.
- Numbers are from a VM whose linear passes cost 2-3 us per 32 KB. Ratios should
  transfer; absolute microseconds should not be compared against the issue's
  macOS arm64 figures.
