## Accomplished

### Issue #164 — density guard on the sparse-rhs entry points (7f1682e)

`LuParams::sparse_rhs_max_density`, default `0.10`. Past that fraction of `m` the
reach DFS is abandoned mid-walk and the kernel sweeps the whole basis in natural
topological order. `1.0` disables the fallback, `0.0` forces it.
`SparseLu::sparse_rhs_fallbacks()` counts how often it fired.

Not the shape the issue suggested. "Skip the sort and sweep `0..m`" breaks two
invariants: `pattern` is the `O(touched)` reset list restoring `HyperWork`'s
all-zero-between-calls contract (a nonzero written outside it seeds the *next*
solve), and `u_solve_sparse`'s `SingularBasis` guard is deliberately narrowed to
the rows the solution depends on (widening it makes singularity depend on an
unrelated right-hand side's density). So the fallback marks the whole accumulator
and fills `order` with `0..m`; the four kernels are untouched, and a fallen-back
solve *is* the dense entry point, guard width included. Full argument in
`dev/decisions.md`.

### Issue #165 — the ordering becomes a parameter (4a83bab)

`SparseLuSymbolic::analyze_with(a, LuOrderingParams { triangularize })`. Both
existing constructors kept as thin wrappers; `LuOrderingParams::default()` is
`triangularize: false`, so the default is unchanged. Python gains `triangularize`
(default `None` = follow `dense_bump_max_dim > 0`, the old coupling) and
`sparse_rhs_max_density`; both keyword-only.

**The AMD-vs-AMF half of #165 is not done.** `feral-amf` is a workspace
dependency but nothing under `src/lu/` references it — AMF is not wired into the
LU at all, so it is separate work, not a second field on this struct. Said so on
the issue rather than closing over it.

### Measurement (255e2b7)

Four arms interleaved in ONE binary (one feral, one discopt), 14 QPLIB
relaxation LPs, 2 reps, best-of, the `api` arm split so the new cap is the only
difference between two of them:

|  | geomean | median | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `apiX/off` — branch as reviewed | 1.066x | 1.083x | 0.735x | 1.405x |
| `api/off` — with the guard | **1.197x** | 1.196x | **0.998x** | 1.408x |
| `api/apiX` — guard alone | 1.122x | 1.122x | 0.929x | 1.393x |

The reviewed arm reproduces (1.066x now against 1.061x then). Every regression is
gone — worst instance 0.998x, parity — and the `alpha >= 10%` bucket goes 0.839x
-> 1.067x. QPLIB_2055 (61.95% dense) goes 0.735x -> 1.024x.

**Better than the 1.116x the review predicted**, because that bound was computed
from the *mean* density per instance while the guard routes *per solve*: dense
individual solves inside nominally-sparse instances get caught too (under-10%
bucket 1.174x -> 1.253x), and the mean-`alpha` column says nothing about the
btran side, which the guard also covers.

Objectives bit-identical across `off`, `apiX`, `api` on all 14 instances. Written
down as an observation, not a guarantee — the tests assert `< 1e-9` between the
routes, not equality.

## Decisions Made

- `sparse_rhs_max_density` is a **separate** knob from `hyper_sparse_max_density`
  despite both defaulting to `0.10`. They cap different things, and keeping them
  apart let `sparse_entry_points_work_with_the_dense_route_disabled` survive as a
  parameterization over both caps rather than an assertion flip.
- The fallback sweeps the whole basis rather than emitting an unsorted reach, so
  it is indistinguishable from the route it falls back to. Both recorded in
  `dev/decisions.md`.
