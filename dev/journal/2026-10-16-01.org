#+TITLE: Session 2026-10-16-01 — review of #153 comment 6223093143 (x86 IPC gap)

* 19:30 :issue-153:review:platform:
The comment (pounce#1020 data) reports a hanging-chain KKT with about the
same instruction count on x86 and aarch64 (215.7M vs 191.2M) but 41-45 ms
on a Cascade Lake KVM guest vs 8.8 ms on an M4 Pro. It asks whether the
rejected levers (infnorm cap/warm start, CB pooling, the #200 0.2 us
per-front constant) still hold on x86. The premise "every rejected lever
was measured on Apple M-series" is false: tried-and-rejected.md:4991 and
:5082 were x86_64 AVX2.

* 19:35 :issue-153:harness:x86:
New crates/feral-diagnostics/src/bin/diag_153_chain_x86.rs builds a 2-D
hanging-chain KKT (10k links, n = 40006, nnz 110010, inertia
(30002,10004,0)). Auto picks Amf + LdltCompress, giving 11875 supernodes
(reporter: 11874), max front 19 (reporter: 20) and nnz_l 355002 (reporter:
366880). So it is a close analogue.
Host: Intel Xeon (family 6 model 207) KVM guest, 4 vCPU, glibc 2.39,
clocksource tsc. An imul-chain calibration gives about 3.2-3.7 GHz effective.

* 19:40 :issue-153:callgrind:scaling:
Callgrind, diff of 4 vs 1 warm refactors: 192.0M Ir per warm refactor
(reporter: 215.7M x86, 191.2M aarch64). Self shares:
compute_infnorm 24.6%, glibc malloc/free 18.4% (+ unlink_chunk 1.1%),
memset/memcpy 8.4%, factor_frontal_blocked 10.0%,
apply_schur_panel_range 6.1%. This matches the reporter's shape.
mc64::compute_matching runs once (cold factor only), and the warm-route
ScalingInfo is Applied with mc64_cache_hit_count 0. So the warm route is
pick_scaling_strategy -> InfNorm. The scaling/mod.rs:458 pre-MC64 InfNorm
trial does NOT fire on this matrix. The lead I raised in the first review
draft was wrong here.
Note: callgrind counts each iteration of rep stosb/movsb as one Ir.
A 320 KB row_max memset costs about 320k Ir. So the x86 Ir total is
inflated relative to aarch64, and the reporter's "implied IPC" overstates
x86 retired instructions.

* 19:50 :issue-153:allocator:bump:
Bump arena (pre-faulted, no-op free), paired alternating, 3 x 60 pairs:
bump/glibc min 1.040 / 1.085 / 1.082, bump wins 15/60, 7/60, 18/60. Bump
is SLOWER. It never reuses memory: 16.2 MB of fresh, cache-cold lines per
refactor. So a bump arena cannot isolate allocator cost. The #200
"bump allocator moved it 0.6-7.7%" result used this instrument too, and
does not rule allocation out.

* 20:10 :issue-153:allocator:sites:
dhat, diff of 4 vs 1 refactors: 93,138 allocs, 16.2 MB per warm refactor
(7.84 / supernode). Every per-front output is re-allocated each refactor:
factor.rs:1835/2237 perm, 1905/2447 l, 1906/2448 d_diag, 1945 contrib
reserve, 1982/2510 perm_inv, 1995/2526 d_subdiag;
factorize.rs:2535 static_rows.to_vec, 2756 contrib_row_indices.
That is 10000 eager-path fronts x 6 + 1875 blocked fronts x 6 + 2 x 11875.
The 2026-05-13 rejection pooled only contribution blocks, on macOS.
Reusing the previous factor's NodeFactors buffers on a pattern-reused warm
refactor is untested.
Implication: on x86 Linux/glibc, allocation is the largest single
measured lever on this matrix (1.7-1.8x via a drop-in allocator). The
rejected-on-Apple allocation verdicts do not transfer.

except through the chain proxies.
