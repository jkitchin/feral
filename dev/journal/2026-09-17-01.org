* 14:35 :issue203:structure:
Read the reproducer's index arithmetic to recover the actual graph.
`NS=775` states per collocation point, `NCP=3` points per element,
`NC=6` controls with one knot per element, `NE=6` path constraints per
point. The embedded spatial coupling `S` is 775x775 with **1977**
nonzeros — 2.55 per row, i.e. a near-tree network (GasLib-40, 10 finite
volumes per pipe), not a dense block.

So the graph is not a chain of dense blocks. It is a **2-D product**:
a sparse spatial network of 775 nodes crossed with a time path of
`3*nfe` points, with the constraint rows roughly doubling each slice.
Spatial extent 775 is *larger* than the temporal extent `3*nfe` for
every size tested (18 at nfe=6, 288 at nfe=96, 648 at nfe=216). The
premise "1-D chain with linear-fill elimination available" does not
hold at these sizes: a time-slice separator is 775 states wide, and
densifying it costs 775^2/2 ~ 3e5 per cut.

The chain ordering *is* exactly linear (2.01x per doubling in fill,
2.02x in flops — the prediction in the issue is arithmetically right)
and it is catastrophic in absolute terms: `front_max` pins at 3299 for
every size, the width of one element's slab. The interleaved variant
changes nothing (79.5M vs 80.5M at nfe=6). Extrapolating both curves,
the crossover is past nfe ~ 1000, far beyond the horizon lengths in
play.

**Conclusion: ask #2 answered, with the opposite sign to what was
expected.** A chain permutation does not recover linear fill in any
useful sense, and a chain-detection heuristic in `Auto` (ask #3) would
be actively harmful on this pattern. Recorded in tried-and-rejected.

* 15:25 :issue203:ordering-gap:evidence:
Isolated the cause. Dumped MA57's pivot order (`KEEP(1:N)` after
MA57AD), inverted it to feral's new-to-old convention, and replayed it
through feral's *own* symbolic pipeline as `External`. Same
amalgamation, same column counts, same everything except the
permutation:

| nfe | feral Auto flops | MA57-METIS perm, feral pipeline | ratio |
|   6 |         5.36e7   |                        8.99e7   | 0.60  |
|  12 |         2.97e8   |                        4.18e8   | 0.71  |
|  24 |         1.83e9   |                        1.72e9   | 1.06  |
|  48 |         1.09e10  |                        6.42e9   | 1.70  |
|  96 |         5.06e10  |                        1.75e10  | 2.89  |

The replayed figures track MA57's own forecast closely (6.42e9 vs
6.68e9 at nfe=48; 1.75e10 vs 1.80e10 at nfe=96), which validates the
cross-solver flop metric and confirms nothing but the permutation is
being compared.

Growth per doubling of `nfe`:
- feral Auto flops: 5.5x, 6.2x, 6.0x, 4.6x  (exponent 2.2-2.6)
- real METIS flops: 4.7x, 4.1x, 3.7x, 2.7x  (exponent falling to ~1.4)

So real METIS *is* bending toward the near-linear asymptote the issue
expects, while every feral backend stays at ~n^2.4. The superlinearity
pounce reports is therefore a genuine ordering-quality deficit — but
in `feral-metis`/`feral-scotch` nested dissection, not in a missing
chain heuristic. `front_max` says the same thing in one number: real
METIS's top separator is 1605, about one time slice of states plus
constraints; feral's is 3204-3453, about twice that.

**This is also the answer to "why is MA57 1.2-1.9x faster per
iteration".** At the sizes pounce runs (nfe=48-216) the ordering gap
alone is 1.7x-2.9x in flops, which covers the reported wall-clock gap
without invoking any kernel difference.
