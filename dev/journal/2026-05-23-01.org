Four decisive findings, all written up in the note:

** F1 - Q4 settled
=Auto= resolves to *ScotchND*, not MetisND as the reporter
believed. =choose_adaptive='s =n > 100_000 && full_avg_deg < 5.0=
branch fires (full_avg_deg = 3.71 from the F1 numbers above).
ScotchND is ~3 s *faster* than explicit MetisND on this matrix,
so re-routing to MetisND would have been worse. Reporter's
hypothesis was based on reading the wrong source line.

** F2 - H1 confirmed; H2 + H3 eliminated
ORDERING stage carries *99.2%* of the wall under all three
orderings. Every feral-side post-pass stage (etree + col_counts +
find_supernodes + permute1/2 + renumber + ...) totals ~1.0 s.
=etree_initial= alone is 13-18 ms at n=2.8M. The feral post-pass
is fast - the time lives entirely in the external ordering crate.

H2 (slow feral post-pass) is dead. The fix has to be in the
external ordering call or in the dispatcher (which method we
pick).

** F3 - AMD wins on fill too, not just on time
=factor_nnz_estimate=: AMD 10.4M, ScotchND 15.8M, MetisND 20.5M.
The chain-pattern catch was *calibrated* on the premise that ND
finds a better separator on low-degree KKT graphs. At n=2.8M with
this topology, the premise is inverted: AMD wins on both axes.

The CHAINWOO calibration (n~3000): AMD 2.10M nnz_L vs MetisND
282k nnz_L (the *original* motivating evidence for the chain
catch). 720× chain-catch payoff at n=3000.

powerflow22 (n=2.8M): AMD 10.4M nnz_L vs MetisND 20.5M nnz_L.
2.0× chain-catch *regression* at n=2.8M.

That's not a quantitative drift - it's a sign flip. The
heuristic is wrong on the powerflow class.

I almost concluded "delete the chain catch entirely" from this.

records the original chain-catch motivation:

#+begin_example
CHAINWOO_0000:
  AMD     sym_nnz_est=68480   num_nnz_l=2,101,584   (cascade!)
  MetisND sym_nnz_est=70552   num_nnz_l=281,526

The chain-catch protects against the *pivoting cascade* that
fattens parent supernodes during numeric BK factorization with
delayed pivots. *Symbolic estimates are nearly identical
(68k ≈ 70k); the 7.5x blowup is numeric-time only.*

My =factor_nnz_estimate=-based tally is the wrong metric. The
catch is doing its job; symbolic prediction just doesn't see it.

Cross-check on powerflow22: reporter measured first factor 113 s,
cached refactor 1.05 s. Cached refactor is pure numeric. 1 s on
2.8M-dim 6.6M-nnz is fast - no cascade. So at n=2.8M with
power-grid topology, cascade doesn't fire and the catch is
harmful. At n~3000-4000 with tridiagonal topology, cascade fires
hard and the catch is load-bearing.

So Fix A is still a *size cap*, not a removal. But the cap has
to be calibrated against where cascade stops firing, which means
I need =num_nnz_l= data, not =factor_nnz_estimate=.
