* 15:20 :issue161:lu:codeinspection:
Read the four triangular kernels in =src/lu/sparse_solve.rs=. They split into
two shapes, and the split explains the issue's headline number exactly:

- =lsolve= (L y = s) and =ut_solve= (U^T z = s) are *scatter* form. They test
  =s[k] == 0.0= and skip, so their flop count is already proportional to the
  solution.
- =usolve= (U w = s) and =lt_solve= (L^T v = s) are *gather* form. Every row
  of U / every column of L is read regardless of what is nonzero:
  =O(nnz(U))= and =O(nnz(L))= on every call.

Issue #161 measured "0.74x the cost for 0.0003x the output". Half the solve
going to ~0 and half staying at full cost predicts ~0.5x-0.75x. Consistent.
The gather halves are the target.

Third finding, not in the issue: =ut_solve= dereferences =u_rows[i]= (one heap
allocation per row) to read =row.first()= *before* testing =s[i]=. So although
its flops are proportional, it pays m cache misses per call regardless of
sparsity. =lsolve= already hoists its zero test; =ut_solve= does not. That is a
one-line fix independent of everything else.

* 15:35 :issue161:lu:design:
The predecessor graphs the reach needs:

- =usolve=: edges =c -> {k : U[k,c] != 0}=. That is exactly =u_above[c]=,
  which already exists (built at =sparse_factor.rs:486=, maintained
  incrementally across FT updates at =sparse_update.rs:236=, and invariant-
  checked in =verify=). Nothing to build.

update — and descending position for lt_solve.

=compute_spike= (=sparse_update.rs:335=) already does this exact shape for the
L graph: mark array, explicit stack, =sort_unstable=, clear marks over the
touched list. Following that idiom rather than inventing a second one.

* 18:40 :issue161:lu:api:design:
Follow-up requested: remove the O(m) floor, adding an API if that is what it
takes. It is: the floor is the =ftran(&mut [f64])= signature, not the kernels.

Added =ftran_sparse= / =btran_sparse=: rhs as (index, value) pairs, solution
appended to a caller-owned =Vec<(usize, f64)>=. Every step is proportional to
nnz or to the reach — scatter O(nnz(rhs)), L-solve over the GP reach, etas
O(ops), U-solve over the reach, gather O(nnz(x) log nnz(x)).

The delicate part is the dense accumulator that must be all-zero between calls
and restored in O(touched). A leak corrupts the *next* solve, not the one that
leaked, so the reset runs on every exit path including the error path out of the
U-solve, and the test interleaves failing solves with good ones rather than
batching them.

Deliberately did NOT skip zero-source eta ops. It would be O(ops) either way,
and skipping diverges from =FtEta::apply_forward= when mult is non-finite
(mult * 0.0 = NaN, not 0.0). Bit parity with the dense path is worth more than
a branch that changes no asymptotics.

* 19:05 :issue161:lu:measurement:scalability:
Measured, bump=0 band=3, unit-vector rhs, ftran p50:

  m       dense sweep   reach-limited   sparse API   work (scalar ops)
  1000       7.8 us        4.7 us        0.30 us     12.5
  4000      40.8 us       20.3 us        1.07 us     12.1
  16000    183.4 us       89.6 us        2.47 us     13.1
  64000   1251.0 us      616.7 us        3.34 us     11.0

*The work column is the result.* m grows 64x, the operation count does not move.
The dense sweep grows 160x, which is what O(m) looks like. At m=64000 the sparse
API is 277x the dense sweep on ftran, 234x on btran.

Time still grows 11x over that 64x range. That is NOT a residual O(m) term — it
decelerates (4x in m buys 2.3x in time from 4k->16k, 1.35x from 16k->64k), which
is a cache/TLB plateau. The accumulator is 512 KB at m=64000, past L2, so each
of the ~12 scattered accesses is a miss. O(work x miss cost), not O(m).

This is exactly why =last_sparse_solve_work()= is asserted on instead of a
timing. A reintroduced O(m) term at these sizes would look like a constant
factor, indistinguishable from noise. The counter is deterministic.

* 23:20 :issue161:mechanism:wrong:
*The mechanism I gave for the regression was wrong, in my PR and in the research
note.* Both blamed the O(r log r) sort.

Sorting 400/1000/2000 usizes measures 2.8/9.0/18.3 us. The regression is ~105 us
per solve. The sort is at most a sixth of it.

Instrumented the reach to count graph edges walked (temporary; reverted).
QPLIB_1157 btran:

  cap    fired     nodes/sweep   DFS edges/sweep   vs nnz(L)=75084
  0.10   21/256    2             3662              5%
  0.25   256/256   584           68925             92%

*The reach DFS traverses the same factor entries the numeric sweep then
traverses again.* At 0.25 it walks 92% of nnz(L) just to decide which columns to
visit, then visits them. ~1.9x to avoid 1x, predicting ~0.53x against a measured
0.62x.

This reframes the cap and I had it backwards. It is not a workaround for an
implementation detail a cleverer sweep could remove — it is intrinsic to
reach-based solving: *finding* the reach is the same order of work as the solve,
so the route can only win when the reach is a small fraction of the whole.
Replacing the sort would not have helped, and the "known limit" I listed pointed
at the wrong thing. Corrected in the param doc, CHANGELOG, PR body, research
note, and the pinning test's comment.

Worth keeping: I had a plausible mechanism, it was in my own "known limits"
list, and it was wrong. Cost 20 minutes to actually measure and it changed the
conclusion from "a lower cap is a workaround" to "a lower cap is the design".
