* 15:20 :issue161:lu:codeinspection:
Read the four triangular kernels in =src/lu/sparse_solve.rs=. They split into
two shapes, and the split explains the issue's headline number exactly:

- =lsolve= (L y = s) and =ut_solve= (U^T z = s) are *scatter* form. They test
  =s[k] == 0.0= and skip, so their flop count is already proportional to the
  solution.
- =usolve= (U w = s) and =lt_solve= (L^T v = s) are *gather* form. Every row
  of U / every column of L is read regardless of what is nonzero:
  =O(nnz(U))= and =O(nnz(L))= on every call.

Issue #161 measured "0.74x the cost for 0.0003x the output". Half the solve
going to ~0 and half staying at full cost predicts ~0.5x-0.75x. Consistent.
The gather halves are the target.

Third finding, not in the issue: =ut_solve= dereferences =u_rows[i]= (one heap
allocation per row) to read =row.first()= *before* testing =s[i]=. So although
its flops are proportional, it pays m cache misses per call regardless of
sparsity. =lsolve= already hoists its zero test; =ut_solve= does not. That is a
one-line fix independent of everything else.

* 15:35 :issue161:lu:design:
The predecessor graphs the reach needs:

- =usolve=: edges =c -> {k : U[k,c] != 0}=. That is exactly =u_above[c]=,
  which already exists (built at =sparse_factor.rs:486=, maintained
  incrementally across FT updates at =sparse_update.rs:236=, and invariant-
  checked in =verify=). Nothing to build.
- =lt_solve=: edges =i -> {k : L[i,k] != 0}= — the row-wise structure of L.
  L is stored CSC only. Must be built. Safe to build once at factor time
  because the FT update never touches the base L (documented at
  =sparse_factor.rs:41=).

Topological order: descending =uperm[k]= (triangular rank) for usolve —
*not* position index, since U is upper triangular in uperm order after an FT
update — and descending position for lt_solve.

=compute_spike= (=sparse_update.rs:335=) already does this exact shape for the
L graph: mark array, explicit stack, =sort_unstable=, clear marks over the
touched list. Following that idiom rather than inventing a second one.

Research note written to =dev/research/hyper-sparse-solves-2026-08-13.md=
before touching any code, per protocol.

* 16:40 :issue161:lu:bug:tests:
The sweep counter earned its keep on the first run. All nine differential
tests passed on correctness and =reach_route_agrees_with_dense_route_on_unit_rhs=
failed on the vacuity assertion:

  the reach route must have fired on most of the 400 ftran + 400 btran
  sweeps, got 0

Cause: =reach_cap()= tested =self.reach.mark_len() != self.m= to decide whether
the workspace was live — but =ftran_core= =mem::take=s the workspace out of
=self= for the duration of the call, so =self.reach= is the empty =Default= at
exactly the moment the test runs. The route was disabled on every solve and
every correctness test still passed, because the dense fallback is correct.

That is the whole argument for =hyper_sparse_sweeps()= existing. Without it
this ships as a no-op with a green suite.

Fixed by testing the taken-out =rw= instead.

* 17:30 :issue161:lu:negative:
Chased the residual O(m). A hyper-sparse ftran at m=4000 still costs ~22 us with
a reach of 3.4 positions, so I hypothesised the four permuted gather/scatters
around the core solve (all m accesses are random through a permutation) and
replaced them with fill + sequential scan + one random access per nonzero.

It does nothing. A/B toggling only that gate:

  sparse marshal   ftran 33.6  btran 31.6  fallback 0.90x
  dense  marshal   ftran 31.3  btran 33.0  fallback 0.93x

The arms straddle each other. Reverted; written up in tried-and-rejected.md.

The hypothesis was wrong because the permuted access is random *within 32 KB*,
which is L1/L2-resident — it was never paying the misses I assumed. A phase
probe shows the residual spread evenly over ~6 linear passes:

  loop base (fill)                 0.2 us
  ftran_partial (P-gather+lsolve) 10.3 us
  ftran - ftran_partial           13.2 us
  full ftran                      22.1 us

There is no single term left. Getting under the O(m) floor needs a sparse-rhs
entry point, i.e. an API addition, not a cheaper walk of a dense one.
