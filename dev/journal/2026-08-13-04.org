* 20:00 :issue164:design:
Read both solve paths before designing. The dense route
(=sparse_solve.rs=) already has the shape the sparse route needs: =reach_cap()=
turns =hyper_sparse_max_density= into a node budget, and =ReachWork::push=
*abandons the DFS the moment it exceeds the budget* rather than completing a
reach it is going to throw away. The sparse route (=sparse_hyper.rs=) has no
such bound anywhere.

First design (issue #164's suggestion, "skip the sort and sweep 0..m") does not
survive contact with the code:

- the sweep must not write nonzeros into positions absent from =pattern= --
  that is the =O(touched)= reset list, and a nonzero outside it silently seeds
  the *next* solve. So a dense sweep has to mark as it writes, or mark
  everything up front.
- =u_solve_sparse='s singular guard is deliberately *narrowed* to rows the
  solution depends on (decisions.md 2026-08-13). Sweeping all =m= rows widens
  it, which would make =SingularBasis= fire or not depending on the density of
  an unrelated right-hand side. Trying to keep the narrowing meant a per-row
  "does this position matter" test that needs the reach I just abandoned.

Settled on a smaller shape instead: the four kernels already sweep whatever is
in =hw.order=, so the fallback only has to *fill =order= differently* -- natural
topological order over =0..m= (ascending for =L=, descending for =Lt=,
descending/ascending =uperm_inv= rank for =U=/=Ut=) instead of the sorted
reach -- and mark the whole accumulator so the reset list covers the sweep.
The kernels themselves are untouched, and the fallback's behaviour is then
*exactly* the dense entry points', guard width included, which is the thing it
is falling back to.

Cost of marking everything: reset becomes O(m) and the next kernel's DFS
trips the cap immediately. Both are O(m) in a regime where the answer is
already dense, and =over_cap= is monotone within a solve so it costs one
pop, not a re-walk.
